# A Personal Journal: Finding Balance and Building Better Habits

## About Me

My name is Alex, and I am a 22-year-old college student. I live with my family in a peaceful town. I enjoy spending time with people I trust, listening to music, going for evening walks, and discovering new places. I am generally a quiet person when meeting new people, but I become talkative once I feel comfortable. I value honesty, kindness, independence, and keeping promises.

I believe that a meaningful life is not only about achieving big goals. It is also about appreciating ordinary moments, maintaining healthy relationships, and learning something from difficult experiences. Over the past year, I have started paying more attention to how I spend my time and what makes me feel satisfied at the end of the day.

## My Daily Routine

On weekdays, I usually wake up at 6:30 AM. I drink a glass of water, freshen up, and spend about ten minutes planning my day. I leave for college at around 8:00 AM. My classes generally finish by 4:00 PM, and I reach home around 4:45 PM.

After returning home, I take a short break and have a snack. I try to spend the next hour completing assignments or reviewing what I learned during the day. Around 6:30 PM, I go for a walk in the nearby park. Walking helps me clear my mind, especially after a busy day.

I have dinner with my family at approximately 8:00 PM. After dinner, I usually read a few pages of a book, listen to music, or prepare for the next day. I aim to sleep by 10:45 PM, although I sometimes stay awake longer when I have unfinished work.

My biggest routine problem is using my phone for too long before sleeping. I sometimes plan to check messages for five minutes but end up scrolling for forty minutes. This makes it harder to wake up refreshed the next morning.

## My Family and Relationships

My family is an important part of my life. We may disagree about small things, but I appreciate the support we give one another. On Sundays, we usually have lunch together without rushing. Sometimes we prepare a special meal at home, and everyone helps with a different task.

I have two close friends whom I have known for several years. One is very practical and helps me think clearly when I am worried. The other is cheerful and can make an ordinary day feel enjoyable. I appreciate that I can speak honestly with both of them without pretending to be someone else.

I have learned that strong friendships require time, trust, and communication. If a friend seems distant, I try not to immediately assume that something is wrong. People have their own responsibilities and difficult days. At the same time, I believe it is important to talk openly when a problem continues instead of silently building resentment.

One thing I want to improve is expressing appreciation more often. I sometimes assume that people already know I care about them. I am learning that a simple message, a sincere thank-you, or spending time together can make that appreciation clearer.

## My Interests and Favorite Activities

I enjoy listening to music while travelling or relaxing. I prefer calm songs when I am tired and energetic music when I need motivation. Music helps me change my mood, but I also recognize that listening to music alone does not solve the problems that caused a bad mood.

I like evening walks because they give me time away from screens. My favorite route passes through a small park with large trees and a pond. I usually walk for thirty minutes. When the weather is pleasant, I stay a little longer and watch families, children, and older people enjoying the park.

I also enjoy reading personal stories and biographies. I like learning how people respond to setbacks, make difficult choices, and gradually improve their lives. I do not expect every story to provide a perfect answer to my own problems, but I often find one useful idea to think about.

My favorite day of the week is Sunday because it gives me more freedom. I can wake up later, spend time with family, visit a nearby market, or simply relax at home without following a strict schedule.

## My Current Goals

I have three main goals for the next six months.

My first goal is to improve my physical well-being. I want to walk for at least thirty minutes five days a week and drink more water during the day. I do not want to follow an extreme plan that becomes difficult to maintain. I would rather build a simple routine that I can continue even when college becomes busy.

My second goal is to become more organized. I plan to write down my three most important tasks every morning. Instead of trying to finish everything at once, I want to complete the most important tasks first. I will review my progress every Sunday evening and adjust my plans for the following week.

My third goal is to save money for a short family trip. I would like to visit a quiet hill station with my family during the next holiday. My target is to save 12,000 rupees over six months. I plan to save 2,000 rupees each month by reducing unnecessary purchases and tracking my spending. I have not chosen the destination yet because I want everyone in the family to contribute ideas.

## A Difficult Week

Two months ago, I had a particularly difficult week. I had several assignments due close together, an unexpected family responsibility, and very little time to relax. I tried to manage everything by staying awake late and skipping my evening walks.

Initially, I thought working longer hours would help me catch up. Instead, I became tired, distracted, and impatient. I started making small mistakes and found it difficult to concentrate. I also cancelled plans with my friends because I felt guilty about taking a break.

By Thursday evening, I realized that my approach was not working. I wrote down everything that needed to be completed and divided the work into smaller tasks. I asked a classmate to clarify one assignment requirement rather than spending hours guessing. I also explained my situation to my family and asked for a little more uninterrupted time.

I completed the most urgent assignments first and moved less important tasks to the weekend. I resumed my evening walks, even though I could only walk for fifteen minutes that week. By Sunday, I felt more in control.

That experience taught me that being productive does not mean working every possible minute. Planning realistically, asking for help, resting when necessary, and choosing priorities can be more effective than simply working longer.

## A Memorable Family Day

Last December, my family and I visited a botanical garden outside our town. We left home at 7:00 AM and reached the garden before 9:00 AM. The weather was cool, and the garden was quiet during the early morning.

We spent several hours walking along paths lined with flowers and tall trees. My mother enjoyed photographing the flowers, while my father was interested in the garden's older trees. We brought homemade food and ate lunch at a shaded picnic area.

On the way home, we stopped at a small roadside shop and bought tea and snacks. Nothing extraordinary happened that day, but everyone was relaxed, and we had long conversations without checking our phones constantly.

I remember that trip because it reminded me that enjoyable experiences do not always need expensive hotels or elaborate plans. Sometimes a simple outing, shared food, and uninterrupted time with people you love are enough to create a lasting memory.

## Lessons I Am Learning

I am learning not to compare my progress constantly with other people's progress. Everyone has different responsibilities, resources, and starting points. Comparing myself with someone else can occasionally inspire me, but it can also distract me from the improvements I have already made.

I am also learning that motivation is unreliable. On some days, I feel enthusiastic about my goals. On other days, I would rather do nothing. Small habits help me continue even when I do not feel particularly motivated.

Another lesson is that asking for help is not a sign of weakness. I used to believe I should solve every problem independently. Now I understand that seeking advice can save time and help me see possibilities I had missed.

Finally, I want to make more time for the people and activities that matter to me. I do not want to spend so much time planning a better future that I forget to appreciate the life I have today.

## My Plans for Next Month

Next month, I plan to make three practical changes. First, I will keep my phone away from my bed and stop scrolling at least thirty minutes before sleeping. Second, I will prepare a weekly schedule every Sunday evening, including study time, exercise, family time, and rest. Third, I will contact one friend each week to arrange a conversation or a simple outing.

At the end of the month, I will review what worked and what did not. I will not consider the month a failure if I miss a few days. Instead, I will look for patterns, identify obstacles, and make adjustments.

My long-term aim is to build a balanced life in which personal growth, family relationships, physical well-being, and enjoyable experiences all have a place. I know that I will not get everything right immediately. For now, I want to focus on making small, consistent improvements and appreciating the progress I make along the way
